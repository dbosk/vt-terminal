\begin{abstract}
Research question: has learners' understanding of the Unix shell, the command line, the user-level file system (directories, paths, folders) or SSH/remote shells been MEASURED -- by a phenomenographic or interview study of learners' conceptions, by a validated diagnostic instrument or concept inventory, or by a knowledge pre-test/post-test? And what do the existing empirical studies of shell/command-line teaching actually measure (understanding vs. perception/confidence/satisfaction surveys vs. command-log behaviour)? A secondary question: method precedents for a pre-test/post-test on critical aspects in learning studies (variation theory, Marton, Pang, Kullberg) and the typology of diagnostic instruments for misconceptions (interviews, open-ended tests, multiple-choice, multi-tier tests, concept inventories) in any discipline. KEEP a paper if it (a) studies learners'/students'/novices' conceptions, misconceptions, mental models or understanding of the Unix/Linux shell, command line, terminal, file system hierarchy, or SSH, by any method; (b) presents or reviews a diagnostic instrument or concept inventory for computing topics (shell, OS, CS1), or a review of concept inventories in computing; (c) evaluates a shell/command-line/terminal teaching intervention or workshop (e.g. Software Carpentry) and reports what was measured, even if only perception or confidence; (d) analyses command logs / terminal histories of students; (e) is a learning-study or variation-theory paper describing pre-/post-tests and critical aspects; (f) reviews diagnostic-instrument types for misconceptions across a discipline. DISCARD papers on other domains (chemistry, physics, biology, nursing, psychology, medicine, management), general software tools or manuals that merely mention a command line, psychometric scale development unrelated to education in computing, generic self-efficacy studies not about the command line, and single-topic science diagnostic tests (two-tier tests on e.g. acids, circuits) unless they are reviews of instrument types. The search covered openalex, dblp, scopus and yielded 522 papers (32 kept, 490 discarded, 0 pending).
\end{abstract}
